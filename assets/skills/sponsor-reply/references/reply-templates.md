# Reply templates and standing terms

Drafts only — Lucas sends his own mail. Match his register: warm, direct, short.
He opens with "Sup <name>" or "Hi <name>", closes with "Cheers", uses a smiley,
and never writes "I hope this email finds you well". The quote block itself gets
formal and itemised; the words around it stay casual.

## Standing terms — attach to every quote

- Final creative and editorial control stays with Lucas. Script/draft review is
  one round, for factual accuracy and brand alignment only.
- Only claims about products he's actually tested. Sponsorship disclosure is
  included and non-negotiable.
- Editing rights are scoped to the brand's own ad placements, not the channel
  video. Edited versions must not misrepresent his statements or be attributed to
  him out of context.
- Usage rights and exclusivity are term-limited; renewals negotiated separately.
- Payment: 50% on signature, 50% on publication. Net 15. Invoiced from his
  Portuguese company; EU reverse-charge VAT applies.
- Before contracting, ask for: agency name and website, a draft agreement, and a
  brand-side reference contact.

```
Lucas
```

## Template B — decline, affiliate-only

```
Sup <name>!
Sorry for the delay on the response but I get quite a few of these.

I'm not taking affiliate only type deals so for this one I'll have to pass!

Have a good week :)

Cheers
```

If the tool is genuinely interesting, add one line saying so and leave the door
open on a base-rate basis — several sponsors have come back with budget after
exactly that.

## Template C — identity check

```
Sup <name>,

This could be a collab but could you reach out to me on LinkedIn through a
validated account? These burner emails raised a little red flag with me.

Cheers
```

## Template D — holding the price against a lowball

```
Hi <name>,

Appreciate you being straight about the budget. <Amount> doesn't work for the
scope as described — but here's what does at that number:

• <reduced scope: shorter segment, or no usage rights, or no cross-post>

If you'd rather keep the full scope, my number stays <original>. Either works
for me, just let me know which one you want.

Cheers,
Lucas
```

The point of D is that the price per unit of value never moves. Scope moves.

## Template E — decline, poor fit

```
Sup <name>,

Thanks for reaching out. I had a look at <product> — <one specific, honest
sentence from the research>. It's just not close enough to what my audience
comes to the channel for, so I'd be doing you a disservice taking this one.

Good luck with it :)

Cheers
```

Being specific costs nothing and is the reason good sponsors come back later with
something that does fit.
